## Core Ideas

- An embedded system is a computer built into a larger product to perform a dedicated function, rather than a general-purpose PC.
- Typical design constraints: limited memory and processing, low power, real-time deadlines, cost sensitivity, and long product lifetimes.
- Real-time behaviour is about meeting deadlines predictably; distinguish hard, firm, and soft real-time requirements.
- Hardware/software co-design: functionality can move between silicon and firmware depending on cost, power, and timing.
- The development toolchain: cross-compiler, linker, programmer/debugger, and the target board (AUT AT90USB1287 lab board for this course).
