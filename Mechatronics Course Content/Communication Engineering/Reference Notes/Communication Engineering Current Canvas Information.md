## Course structure

ENEL700 covers analogue and digital communication systems, signal analysis and conversion, modulation and demodulation, transmitters and receivers, noise, information theory, coding, networking, and modern telecommunication technology. Canvas describes 12 weekly lecture/tutorial sessions and recommends budgeting about 12 hours per week. The schedule lists Tuesday lectures, Thursday tutorials, and three lab streams, but students are told to confirm current times and venues in MyAUT.

## Current Lecture 1–2 preparation

- [Lecture 1](https://canvas.aut.ac.nz/courses/23148/pages/overview) introduces transmitter–channel–receiver systems, modulation, multiplexing, duplexing, spectrum, frequency, wavelength, bandwidth, and communication applications. Before class, review Canvas resources, assessments, the study calendar, and engineering mathematics.
- [Lecture 2](https://canvas.aut.ac.nz/courses/23148/pages/overview-2) reviews gain, attenuation, decibels, tuned circuits, filters, Fourier theory, and signal bandwidth. Canvas asks students to recap Lecture 1 and electrical-engineering fundamentals before class.

## Lab preparation resources

The 23 July [lab-resources announcement](https://canvas.aut.ac.nz/courses/23148/discussion_topics/554831) provides preparatory videos for amplitude modulation (Lab 1), frequency modulation (Lab 2), binary phase-shift keying (Lab 3), pulse-code modulation (Lab 4), Simulink amplitude modulation (Lab 5), and Fourier/harmonic analysis (Lab 6). Use those links as preparation rather than as replacements for the lab manual.

Keep a **hardcopy logbook** during every lab. Record measured data, waveforms, observations, errors or mistakes, and how they were resolved; Canvas says the logbook will be checked in Week 12.
